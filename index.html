What I actually do

Three things, in the order they show up in a working week.

Keep people working

Diagnose and fix Windows and Linux machines, network faults, access problems and failing hardware — onsite and remote — and write the ticket up so the next person does not start from zero.

Watch for what looks wrong

Collect authentication and system logs into a SIEM, build dashboards that surface the events worth a look, and work an alert through to a decision instead of a shrug.

Remove the manual step

Turn repeated work into small tools — an inventory database instead of a notebook, a scripted workflow instead of a ritual — and check the result against the old way.

How to read the numbers here

I use three kinds of labels on this site, and they mean different things:

  • Job result — a figure from work I was paid to do, taken from my own records. Real, self-reported, and not independently audited. The baseline and measurement window are available on request.
  • Lab exercise — something I built and ran to practise, in a home environment. It demonstrates method, not client impact.
  • Tested — a number produced by running code in front of you, which you can reproduce. The log-triage lab is the only place that applies.

I don't publish a figure for work I can't evidence. Where a number belongs to a job and not to a project, I say which job.
