IT Support Case Study — Clearwox Systems

Field support across client sites: fixing the fault in front of me, then writing it down so the next person does not start from nothing.

Context

Clearwox Systems sent me to client sites, which meant the first hour of most days was triage in the literal sense: work out what was broken, how many people it was stopping, and whether it could be fixed from where I was standing. The work covered Windows machines, network connectivity, access problems and dying hardware — the unglamorous middle of IT support where a lot of a company's day quietly disappears.

What the job actually involved

  • Diagnosing hardware and software faults on Windows desktops and laptops — boot failures, driver conflicts, failing drives, corrupted profiles.
  • Tracing network connectivity problems — cabling, switch ports, IP conflicts, DNS, and the usual gap between “the internet is down” and what is actually happening.
  • Resolving access problems — account lockouts, permissions, credential resets, and the verification conversation that has to happen before any of that.
  • Running system checks, updates and maintenance so that the same machine did not come back next week.
  • Supporting users remotely and onsite, depending on what the fault allowed.
  • Documenting tickets accurately and escalating when a problem was outside what I could safely fix.

How I worked a fault

  1. Reproduce it before touching it. Half of support is refusing to accept “it's broken” as a description. I wanted the exact symptom, the last change made, and whether it happened for one person or everyone.
  2. Establish the blast radius. One machine is a user problem. A shared drive or a switch is an office problem. That determines whether I work alone or call it in immediately.
  3. Change one thing at a time. Fastest-looking fixes are usually the ones that make the next diagnosis impossible.
  4. Verify with the user, not with the log. A service that reports healthy while the user still cannot print is not fixed.
  5. Write it down. Symptom, cause, fix, and what to check first next time — because the second occurrence should be cheaper than the first.

Results

Across the period, support resolved 95% of the issues it took on without needing a repeat visit, and the proactive upgrade and optimisation work reduced downtime by 30%. Tailored IT solutions for client sites improved operational efficiency by about 20%.

What I'd do differently now

  • Instrument it. Those figures come from my own records, and I'd rather they came from a ticket system with consistent categories — first-contact resolution is a much stronger claim when it is counted automatically.
  • Build the knowledge base as I go, not after. Three clients hit the same printer driver problem; that should have been a one-page fix by the second one.
  • Tag root causes, not just symptoms, so patterns show up before they become trends.

On the numbers: 95%, 30% and 20% are self-reported from my own records at Clearwox Systems (2021). They are not independently audited. If you want to interrogate them, ask me for the baseline and the period — I will show you what I have and say plainly where the record is thin.
